§ 1

The evidentiary class

Decision-sequence evidence is evidence sufficient to reconstruct designated human and automated decision states and their ordering. It is the missing class in human-AI oversight: system logs record what a machine did, and attestations record what a person says they did, but neither can establish that an independent judgment existed before the machine's output was seen.

Exposure boundary: the boundary separating a committed pre-exposure decision state from specified AI information. A judgment recorded as a pre-exposure commitment crosses it exactly once, and any later change is an AI-associated revision, recorded without causal attribution.

DSES is an open specification representing this class. Anyone may implement it without permission. Sequence evidence is liability-direction-neutral: the same record can support the clinician, the vendor, or the institution, depending on what happened. That neutrality is why adversarial parties can share one substrate.

§ 2

Verification, not assurance language

The specification classifies every one of its requirements by how it can be established: by schema, by cryptography, by cross-record recomputation, by statistics, or only by attestation. The reference verifier recomputes everything in the first four classes from the evidence package alone, and the release gate fails the build if any implemented claim lacks a verifier rule and an adversarial fixture that attacks it.

The attestation boundary is published, not hidden: what no verifier can mechanically establish (adjudicator conduct, key custody, downstream use) is named as such, and a conformance claim must not assert it.

§ 3

Conformance

DSES-compatible is self-declared and free. DSES Conformant requires passing the published gate for a named version and filing the verification transcript in the public registry, so every conformance claim is checkable against a named transcript: the standard applied to its own name.

§ 4

What DSES refuses to establish

A statistic must not outrun its evidence. By construction, no DSES artifact or conformance claim may assert:

  • a standard of care, or a bounded definition of reasonable AI reliance;
  • a competence, negligence, or adverse-action determination about any professional;
  • a normative threshold derived from an observed reference distribution;
  • legal privilege, admissibility, or exemption from lawful discovery.

The schema prohibits the fields and the verifier rejects the artifacts. Individual-level derivation requires prespecified, anchored governance: declared purpose, minimum cell sizes, mandatory intervals, and a balanced reliance context that cannot be stripped from the rate it conditions.
